The law

Three sentences that decide the design.

1. Atoms where

Simple

Making a thing is answering one question over and over: which tiny bit of stuff goes in which spot? Drawings, toolpaths, and robots are different ways of answering. We answer with fields from the walls.

Technical

Manufacturing as a placement field on a bounded volume. Geometry + material + interior state compressed into boundary-measurable chords where possible. Information ladder: functional description ≪ chord pattern ≪ fine voxels ≪ atomic microstate (transporter — ruled out).

2. Boundary control

Simple

If the walls are the tool, you do not need hands inside. Hands inside would have to be built too — and then the machine that builds machines gets stuck building hands forever.

Technical

Self-replication favors a small set of surface primitives (plates, panels, feedstock) over kinematic complexity. Moving parts inside the volume create a dependency loop. Enclosure motion for access is allowed; in-volume manipulators are not Plan A.

3. The Replicator builds the Replicator

Simple

Only the first one is allowed to be hard and handmade. After it can place atoms, it makes the parts for the next one. Each generation can be bigger, finer, or cheaper — because the factory is the product.

Technical

Organizing constraint, not a near-term demo claim. Implies: (1) parts must be expressible as field-assembled feedstock patterns; (2) software interface must survive the jump to electronics (MachineBackend); (3) first v0 is conventional once; (4) “v1 built by v0” is gated on assemble working with receipts — not a slogan ahead of physics.

What we demote

Ideas that fight the law or outrun evidence stay off the kid pages and off the product sentence:

  • Transmutation / alchemy / “creating gold” as product
  • Optical supercomputer / holographic RAM as shipped subsystems
  • Four-field marketing (sound/light/microwave/magnet as four “hands”)
  • Skyrmion / flying-doughnut / anapole language on public simple pages
  • Treating gated simulation as a measured machine

Deep research can still live in papers. This book stays honest.
